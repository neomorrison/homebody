homebody

A house robot you can build for about $1,196, trying real chores in a simulated copy of its owner's apartment. These are scripted attempts, recorded straight from the physics sim. The ones marked "Not yet" are what the learned brain has to fix.

The apartment

The rooms are rebuilt from an iPhone LiDAR scan: walls, doorways and furniture are measured from the scan, and the floor and rug textures are cut from its photos. It is a clean recreation rather than the raw scan mesh, so colours, lighting and the movable furniture can change every episode. That keeps a camera-based brain from memorising one exact picture of the room.

The robot

BaseTwo hub motors from a used hoverboard, sprung casters, anti-tip outrigger wheels, 12 kg steel ballast, a tote on the deck
ReachA counterbalanced lift on a 1.82 m mast (the arm runs from 0.12 to 1.62 m high) and a telescoping arm reaching 0.54 m to the side
HandYaw, pitch and roll wrist with a parallel-jaw gripper on bus servos, and a camera in the palm
SensesLidar, pan/tilt head camera, palm camera, IMU, bumper, cliff sensors, battery monitor, dock beacon, motor encoders
Power36 V 180 Wh Li-ion; backs onto a spring-contact dock to charge itself
BrainRuns on a desktop GPU over Wi-Fi; a Raspberry Pi 5 on board handles motors, sensors and safety

What comes next

  • Teleoperated demonstrations of the grasps that fail here (cloth, paper bag rims, the takeout bag handle), then imitation learning on them.
  • Reinforcement learning for driving, docking from across the apartment and recovering from bumps.
  • Longer chores: loading the washer, moving laundry to the dryer, putting the iron and its cord away.
